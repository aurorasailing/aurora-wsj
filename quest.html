    Where’s this from?

    This is the wind on the water at the Jamboree sailing venue on the Martwa Wisła. It reads the on-water anemometer at Górki Zachodnie (the sailing centre) when it’s reporting, otherwise the modelled wind at the Marina Błotnik point, then Gdańsk — the name under the picture tells you which. The air temperature comes with that reading. The levels are set for an RS Quest — a stable training dinghy that carries a breeze well, so “sporty” runs higher than you’d set for a small single-hander.

    From experience: you might launch in 10 knots and cop 30+ in a gust — you wouldn’t have launched in that, but a reefed Quest with weight aboard stays stable if you keep the sail flat, ease the gnav, and head in. Know your reef point before you go.

    We keep the wind direction simple — one of eight main points: North, North-East, East, South-East, South, South-West, West and North-West.

    Wind from the on-water widget at Górki Zachodnie (Holfuy #1441), the marina point via Open-Meteo, and the coast from IMGW-PIB. Always check conditions on the water before you sail.

    Why the “blowing on the coast” alert?

    The river is sheltered; the open Baltic isn’t. This page also reads the exposed coast (the Baltic Platform, or Hel), and if it’s a band stronger or gusting hard, an amber/red strip appears: calmer on the river, but blowing on the coast. Fine inside the river mouth — but rig for the bigger number before you head out. A separate “very gusty” flag shows when the gust runs well above the average — the lull-then-hit pattern that’s hard to hold.
